# Coaching decision rules

These are defaults for reasoning, not a substitute for the profile. Safety and clinician restrictions take priority. Evidence IDs refer to `EVIDENCE.md`.

## Training

Anchor exercise selection, frequency and volume to goals, experience, availability, equipment and recovery [ACSM-2026]. Cover relevant movement patterns and major muscle groups across the week. Use the smallest sustainable amount of work that moves the goal. An untrained person may need less than an experienced lifter; do not prescribe a universal set count.

Use a workable rep range and RIR target, explain technique and load selection, and prioritize repeatable execution. A practical double-progression heuristic: if all work sets reach the top of the rep range at the intended RIR with good technique on repeated comparable exposures, add the smallest available load increment and work up again. If load jumps are too large, add reps or use a smaller increment. If performance or comfort worsens, inspect recovery and execution before adding work. This is a coaching convention, not a universal scientific threshold.

Complex periodization, routine failure training, and particular equipment are not requirements for the average adult [ACSM-2026]. Use macro/meso/microcycles to organize goals and reviews. Do not invent mandatory shock, detox or muscle-confusion phases. Plan recovery opportunities; deload timing and size should respond to the individual rather than a rigid calendar rule.

Include conditioning and everyday activity within recovery/time constraints. WHO adult population guidance is 150–300 minutes moderate or 75–150 minutes vigorous aerobic activity weekly, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days [WHO-ACTIVITY]. Build toward appropriate levels rather than treating this as a starting prescription for everyone. Avoid prescribing max-effort tests to inexperienced users just to fill a baseline.

## Nutrition

Use habit-based planning when tracking is unwanted, impractical, or inappropriate. For energy estimates, observed intake plus a sufficiently stable multiweek trend can be more informative than a formula, but logs can be biased. The Mifflin equation estimates resting expenditure, not total daily need [MIFFLIN]. An activity multiplier is an uncertain coaching assumption; document it separately and avoid false precision. Do not use a universal calorie floor or automatically prescribe a deficit from BMI alone.

If a suitable adult explicitly seeks fat loss, discuss a modest, sustainable approach adapted to baseline and context, and monitor hunger, performance and recovery. If muscle gain is primary, avoid reflexively prescribing a large surplus. Prefer the smallest change justified by sustained data. Do not turn noisy weight observations into an exact energy-expenditure calculation.

For healthy exercising adults, 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day is a commonly supported protein range [ISSN-PROTEIN]; select an appropriate target in context, not as a minimum for everyone or a treatment recommendation. Consider professional guidance for relevant medical conditions. Distribute protein-containing foods across convenient meals; include carbohydrate to support activity, adequate fat, produce, and variety. WHO recommends adults aim for at least 400 g/day fruit/vegetables and 25 g/day naturally occurring fibre [WHO-DIET]; adapt gradually for tolerance and medical restrictions.

Check food pattern adequacy beyond macros. Ingredient and nutrient claims need sources. Micronutrient supplements should not be prescribed merely because the assistant has incomplete food logs. Use verified clinical advice when a deficiency or special requirement is suspected.

## Reviewing and adapting

- **One difficult day:** consider a smaller session, rest, or a practical meal adjustment. Do not rewrite the entire program.
- **Low adherence:** identify the friction. Reduce complexity or frequency before adding work or restricting food.
- **Consistent execution, good recovery, improving performance:** continue; an unchanged plan can be the correct decision.
- **Repeated plateau:** verify comparable loads/reps/technique and adequate observation length; assess stress, sleep and energy before changing one variable.
- **Weight trend:** optional measurements under similar conditions, robust weekly summaries, multiple weeks, and relevant confounders (water, sodium, menstrual changes, constipation, travel). Missing data is not stability. The CLI's four-days-per-week coverage flag is a transparent product heuristic, not a clinical threshold.
- **Persistent fatigue, recurrent injuries, menstrual disruption, or other possible under-fuelling indicators:** do not deepen restriction or diagnose REDs. Recommend appropriate professional assessment and prioritize adequate fuelling/recovery [IOC-REDS].

Every substantive revision: explain the signal and confidence, change the smallest justified set of variables, specify reassessment timing and reversal conditions, and retain the previous plan.
