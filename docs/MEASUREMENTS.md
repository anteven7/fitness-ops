# What to measure, how, and how to interpret it

Choose the smallest useful set of measurements with the user. Weight, waist, calories, photos and body-fat estimates are not prerequisites for a habit-based program. Never treat unlogged observations as zero or ask someone to track more merely to satisfy the software.

## Measurement guide

| Observation | Repeatable method and units | What it can inform | Main limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body mass | Same scale on a firm level surface, similar clothing and time of day, preferably morning; store kg. Record unusual conditions. | Longer-term mass trends if the user wants them. | Hydration, food/gut contents and other fluctuations; does not distinguish fat, muscle or fluid. |
| Height | Without shoes, upright at a suitable height measure/stadiometer; store cm. | Optional energy-equation input. | Self-measurement error changes the estimate; repeated daily measurement is unnecessary. |
| Waist | If wanted, tape horizontal at the midpoint between the last palpable rib and iliac crest, snug without compressing, at the end of a normal exhalation; store cm and the method. | Change under a consistent protocol. | Site/tension/posture/breathing matter. Not a diagnosis or a body-fat percentage. |
| Sets and reps | Record performed work sets for each named exercise; distinguish warm-up from work. | Comparable performance and adherence. | Technique, range and equipment must be comparable. |
| External load | Record kg, machine/device, per-hand versus total convention, and per-side repetitions in notes. | Progress in the same exercise/setup. | Different machine stacks and exercises are not equivalent; bodyweight work need not invent an external load. |
| RIR | Immediately estimate additional controlled repetitions possible before failure; record uncertainty. | A practical effort guide alongside technique and performance. | Subjective error; avoid forced failure tests to “prove” a number. |
| Session outcome | Completed, partial, or explicitly skipped; link the intended plan/session when possible. | Whether the program fits real life. | Missing log means unknown. Partial sessions are not necessarily failed sessions. |
| Duration | Actual minutes and a consistent convention about warm-up/rest. | Schedule feasibility. | Time is not a direct measure of training stimulus. |
| Food amount | Edible grams with raw/cooked/drained/as-packaged state; use the matching source/label and portion definition. | Approximate nutrient intake and repeatable recipes. | Database/label averages, recipe variation, missing oils/snacks and reporting error. |
| Daily kcal and macros | Complete-day totals from documented portions, or clearly labelled estimates. | Intake patterns when tracking is wanted and appropriate. | Partial meals are excluded from daily averages. Intake is not measured expenditure. |
| Sleep | Estimated sleep duration, not just time in bed; note interruptions/shift work if relevant. | Recovery context and routine feasibility. | A watch or self-report is an estimate; duration does not establish quality or diagnose a disorder. |
| Energy | Subjective 1–5: 1 very low, 3 usual/moderate, 5 very good. Use the same personal anchors. | Within-person changes and discussion. | App convention, not a validated readiness or medical scale. |
| Stress | Subjective 0–10: 0 none, 10 extreme for that person; note context. | Workload and recovery planning. | Ordinal self-report; not comparable between people. |
| Soreness | Subjective 0–10 with body area and impact on movement. | Whether the next session needs adjustment. | Soreness does not establish muscle growth or damage severity. |
| Pain | Subjective 0–10 plus site, onset, provoking activity and persistence. | Whether to stop/adapt and seek assessment. | No number clears exercise or diagnoses injury. Severe/concerning symptoms matter even without a high rating. |

The body-mass method follows [NHS measurement guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/PRN00916-how-to-record-your-weight.pdf). That programme suggests weekly-to-monthly weighing; it does not support a universal daily-weighing requirement. The waist and height methods follow [WHO STEPS, Section 5](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/ncds/ncd-surveillance/steps/part3.pdf?sfvrsn=9bb0f167_2). Food-state/source matching follows [USDA documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/Foundation_Foods_Documentation/). RIR limitations are supported by the [accuracy review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/). Subjective scale anchors above are transparent app conventions.

## Choosing outcomes for the goal

For strength, compare the same exercise, equipment, rep range, technique and approximate effort; inspect the actual sets. For hypertrophy or body composition, combine appropriate longer-term observations with performance and adherence, and acknowledge that these logs do not directly measure muscle size. For general health, consistency, enjoyable activity, function and recovery may matter more than scale change. For any goal, include how manageable and enjoyable the routine is.

A well-supported guideline can suggest useful behavior without validating every metric. Avoid body-composition claims from a photo, a smart-scale fluctuation, or a circumference change. If a medical measurement is needed, use the appropriate professional assessment rather than adding an unvalidated score.

## Averages, noise and red flags

The summary shows denominators and, for observed numerical fields, minima and maxima. These are descriptive. Recovery ratings are ordinal; an average can hide an important bad day, so inspect the worst value and notes. A normal average cannot rule out a concerning symptom.

Interpret mass/waist over enough time to separate plausible trend from day-to-day noise. Relevant context can include travel, hydration, sodium, bowel patterns, menstrual changes or a change in creatine use. Do not infer a cause just because one is possible. Preserve unexpected readings unless the user reports a measurement error; corrections retain the original record.

For general adult sleep guidance, the AASM/SRS consensus recommends at least seven hours regularly for adults aged 18–60, with individual variation. This is not a rule that seven hours guarantees recovery. [Consensus statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26039963/)

Persistent pain, exertional chest pain, fainting, severe breathing difficulty, recurrent injury, or concerning under-fuelling patterns require an appropriate assessment. Do not diagnose REDs from fatigue or a low calorie log. [IOC consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37752011/)
