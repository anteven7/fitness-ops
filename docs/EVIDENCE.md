# Evidence and applicability

Checked 2026-10-21. This is a scoped evidence audit, not a claim to have systematically reviewed every paper or established professional equivalence. Evidence types below describe the sources; they are not invented GRADE ratings. `evidence/sources.json` is the machine-readable register.

## How to use evidence

Use research/guidelines for claims they support in relevant populations. Explain indirect application and uncertainty. Mark a practical starting choice as a heuristic; do not turn it into a scientific fact by appending a citation. A preference does not need a medical paper. For new scientific claims, verify primary sources and add an applicability record before using their ID in an active plan. Record abstract-only access honestly; publication year and search coverage are different dates.

### ACSM-2026

[Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) — 2026; overview of systematic reviews.

Population/context: Healthy adults; resistance training interventions of at least six weeks.

Supports: Progressive resistance training improves muscle function; goals and response guide prescription.

Limits: Does not establish an individually optimal split, RIR target, progression increment, or mandatory periodization. Not rehabilitation.

Access checked: PubMed abstract; publisher summary; full-text retrieval was unavailable (2026-10-21).

### WHO-ACTIVITY

[WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/) — 2020; guideline.

Population/context: Adults, with separate recommendations for specific populations.

Supports: Adult population guidance includes aerobic activity and muscle strengthening.

Limits: Population targets are not a starting prescription for every person.

Access checked: Guideline recommendations (2026-10-21).

### WHO-DIET

[WHO Healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet) — 2026; public health guidance.

Population/context: General population; age-specific considerations.

Supports: Dietary adequacy, diversity, fruit/vegetables and fibre matter beyond macro totals.

Limits: Does not establish personalized energy or sports-nutrition targets.

Access checked: Official guidance (2026-10-21).

### ISSN-PROTEIN

[International Society of Sports Nutrition Position Stand: protein and exercise](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/) — 2017; position stand.

Population/context: Healthy exercising adults.

Supports: Provides a commonly used 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day protein range for exercising adults.

Limits: Not a mandatory minimum for everyone; clinical and body-size context matter.

Access checked: Position stand (2026-10-21).

### MORTON-PROTEIN

[Protein supplementation and resistance-training adaptations: systematic review and meta-regression](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/) — 2018; systematic review and meta-analysis.

Population/context: Healthy adults undertaking prolonged resistance training.

Supports: Supports adequate protein alongside resistance training, with diminishing average gains at higher intakes.

Limits: The estimated breakpoint is uncertain and is not an individual ceiling or proof that supplementation is necessary.

Access checked: PubMed abstract and figure description (2026-10-21).

### MIFFLIN

[A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/) — 1990; prediction-model derivation study.

Population/context: 498 healthy subjects aged 19–78, including normal-weight and obese adults.

Supports: Estimates resting energy from body mass, height, age and an equation coefficient.

Limits: Predictive error and population limits apply; not total expenditure or a calorie prescription.

Access checked: PubMed abstract (2026-10-21).

### HALL-ENERGY

[Quantification of the effect of energy imbalance on bodyweight](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21872751/) — 2011; mathematical modelling study.

Population/context: Adult energy-balance and bodyweight modelling.

Supports: Weight responses to changed intake are dynamic.

Limits: Does not validate converting one week of scale change into an exact energy deficit.

Access checked: Abstract and indexed author paper (2026-10-21).

### RIR-ACCURACY

[Accuracy in Predicting Repetitions to Task Failure in Resistance Exercise](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/) — 2022; scoping review and exploratory meta-analysis.

Population/context: Participants estimating repetitions to task failure in resistance exercise.

Supports: RIR estimates contain error and vary with the exercise and proximity to failure.

Limits: Self-reported effort is not an objective measurement; do not force failure testing to calibrate it.

Access checked: PubMed abstract (2026-10-21).

### WHO-STEPS

[WHO STEPS: Part 3, Section 5, physical measurements](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/ncds/ncd-surveillance/steps/part3.pdf?sfvrsn=9bb0f167_2) — 2017; measurement protocol.

Population/context: Standardized anthropometric surveillance.

Supports: Defines repeatable height, weight and waist measurement methods.

Limits: Self-measurement adds error; circumference is not body-fat percentage or a diagnosis.

Access checked: Official manual, Section 5 (2026-10-21).

### NHS-WEIGHT

[NHS: How to record your weight](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/PRN00916-how-to-record-your-weight.pdf) — date not established; patient measurement guidance.

Population/context: Adults in a weight-management programme.

Supports: Use consistent scales, placement, timing and clothing for repeatability.

Limits: The programme recommends weekly-to-monthly intervals; frequent weighing is optional, not universally better.

Access checked: Official one-page PDF leaflet (2026-10-21).

### USDA-FDC

[USDA FoodData Central Foundation Foods Documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/Foundation_Foods_Documentation/) — 2024; food composition methodology.

Population/context: Laboratory and compiled food composition records.

Supports: Describes food composition, portion calculations, and general versus specific Atwater energy factors.

Limits: Averages and label rounding do not provide exact metabolizable energy for an individual meal/person.

Access checked: Official documentation (2026-10-21).

### IOC-REDS

[IOC consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37752011/) — 2023; consensus statement.

Population/context: Athletes with possible problematic low energy availability.

Supports: Under-fuelling can affect health and performance; concerning patterns need qualified assessment.

Limits: Symptoms are not diagnostic by themselves; the toolkit does not implement a clinical REDs score.

Access checked: PubMed record and abstract (2026-10-21).

### AASM-SLEEP

[Recommended Amount of Sleep for a Healthy Adult](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26039963/) — 2015; consensus statement.

Population/context: Healthy adults aged 18–60.

Supports: Regularly obtaining at least seven hours is the general adult recommendation in this consensus.

Limits: Individual needs differ; hours alone do not establish sleep quality or explain fatigue.

Access checked: Consensus statement (2026-10-21).

### NIH-ODS

[Dietary Supplements for Exercise and Athletic Performance](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/ExerciseAndAthleticPerformance-HealthProfessional/) — living resource; government evidence summary.

Population/context: Users considering performance supplements.

Supports: Ingredient-specific benefits and safety vary; adequate diet and training remain foundational.

Limits: Check the actual ingredient, dose, interactions, product and current evidence before recommending.

Access checked: Official fact sheet (2026-10-21).
