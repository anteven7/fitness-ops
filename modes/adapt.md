# Adapt to current conditions

Read the active plan and the constraint: travel, exams, schedule, equipment, recovery, food access, or symptoms. Identify its duration and the minimum plan that preserves the primary goal.

Preserve useful exercise familiarity and progression history. Match replacement movements by the training purpose, equipment, comfort, and skill; do not claim exact equivalence between machines or dumbbells. Use effort-based loading for a new exercise. Shorten accessories before deleting the highest-priority work. Never make up missed volume all at once.

For travel, include an equipment-free option, realistic session lengths, food-shopping/restaurant choices, and a return plan. For fatigue, inspect recent volume, sleep, stress and nutrition; a deload can reduce work and effort, with timing based on the person's response. For pain, avoid the provoking movement and recommend assessment when persistent/worsening; this is not a rehabilitation protocol.

Version a material change with parent plan ID, dated rationale, temporary scope, return conditions, and review date. Keep unaffected portions of the plan coherent, including nutrition. Activate when the user requested the change. Record the outcome at review rather than assuming the adaptation helped.
